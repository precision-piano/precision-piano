Giving a Piano New Life

A piano can be much more than an instrument. It may be a family heirloom, a remarkable piece of craftsmanship, or an instrument with a musical character worth preserving.

Complete restoration is the process of bringing an instrument's structural, mechanical and musical systems back to a level where the piano can once again perform at its full potential.

With more than four decades of experience restoring and rebuilding pianos, Precision Piano approaches each instrument individually. We examine its condition, history, construction and potential before recommending the work that makes sense.

A Complete Approach

A successful restoration is more than replacing worn parts. Every component of the instrument needs to work together as part of a single musical system.

Depending on the instrument, restoration may include rebuilding the action, restoring or replacing the soundboard and bridges, restringing, replacing the pinblock, restoring dampers and pedals, refinishing the cabinet, and carefully regulating and voicing the completed instrument.

Our goal is to preserve what makes each piano special while bringing its structure, mechanics and musical response back to the highest level the instrument can achieve.

The Restoration Process

Every piano follows its own path. The exact work depends on the instrument, its condition and the goals of its owner.

1

Evaluation

We examine the piano's structure, action, soundboard, bridges, strings, pinblock, case and other major components to understand its condition and potential.

2

Planning

We determine what work is necessary, what can be preserved, and what approach will best serve the instrument and its intended use.

3

Rebuilding

Structural and mechanical work is carried out with careful attention to fit, materials, tolerances and craftsmanship.

4

Musical Preparation

Once the major work is complete, the piano is carefully regulated, tuned and voiced so its mechanical precision translates into musical expression.

Is Restoration Right for Your Piano?

Not every older piano needs a complete restoration, and not every piano is a good candidate for one. The condition and construction of the instrument, its musical potential, its history and the goals of its owner all matter.

An evaluation can help determine whether a piano would benefit from restoration, more limited rebuilding, or simply careful ongoing service.
